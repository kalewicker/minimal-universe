# R06a — analytical observable and hypothesis design

**Analytical audit: pass. Recommendation: edge-budget allocation for a separate
calibration. Production metric adoption: none. Scientific status: untested.**

The [source-bound protocol](../protocols/r06a.json) and
[candidate/control definitions](../protocols/r06a-observable-design.json) were
registered before the exclusive `r06a-001` execution. This stage follows R05g's
readiness review and asks a new, limited question: how much of a nonempty graph's
edge budget supports distinct reciprocal relations beyond what its degree
sequence predicts? It does not revise the meaning of an earlier effect estimate.

Let R count unordered distinct reciprocal vertex pairs, E all edge occurrences,
S distinct nonloop directed arcs, L nonloop occurrences, and M nonloop occurrences
with reverse support. The candidates and prespecified analytical criteria were:

| Candidate | Formula | Bounded | Defined for every nonempty control, including loops | Isolate invariant | Reciprocal-copy invariant | Total nonempty exact null |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Historical pair density | R / choose(N,2) | Yes | No | No | No | No |
| Edge-budget allocation | 2R / E | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Support fraction | 2R / S | Yes | No | Yes | Yes | No |
| Occurrence fraction | M / L | Yes | No | Yes | Yes | No |

The fixed policy recommends every candidate passing every criterion for
subsequent calibration, retaining ties or no-candidate outcomes. It therefore
recommends **2R/E** among these four candidates. The criteria express the stated
edge-allocation question; they do not establish a universally best measure of
organization or complexity. Scores were not selected for large observed effects,
favorable laws or favorable saved trajectories.

The audit covered all 791 directed multigraph controls with up to four arcs on
one, two and three vertices, plus the zero-vertex empty graph. Ten named controls
and 18 fixed scaling fixtures produced 819 fixture instances in total. Of these,
816 received exact null enumeration, yielding 3,264 metric/null summaries and
135,086 labelled permutations. Three larger replication fixtures received raw
formula checks only. Fixture families intentionally overlap; these are not
independent parents or a sampled distribution over G2 laws.

Every raw and permutation score matched a separate adjacency-matrix scorer.
Every null preserved the degree sequences, and labelled-stub mass agreed with
an independently structured multiplicity-weighted generator of unique endpoint
sequences. Historical raw density agreed with the unchanged R02 implementation.
Nine supplied-endpoint arithmetic controls and the incomplete two-parent example
preserved initial offsets, signed changes and missing denominators. All 109
Python tests passed.

The normalization behavior follows directly from the definitions:

- With k disjoint reciprocal pairs, pair density is 1/(2k−1), while 2R/E is one.
  At 32 copies, the former is 1/63; the latter remains one.
- Adding isolates leaves E, R and the labelled target-stub null unchanged for
  the budget score. A single reciprocal pair plus isolates at N=64 has pair
  density 1/2,016 and budget allocation one.
- Duplicating both reciprocal arcs m times reduces 2R/E to 1/m. Adding q loops
  reduces it to 2/(2+q). Support/occurrence fractions stay one in these controls.
  This is an explicit cost assigned to redundancy and loops, which may be
  useful structure under other hypotheses.

Each reciprocal pair needs at least two distinct nonloop occurrences, so
0 <= 2R/E <= 1 whenever E>0. A degree-conditioned null preserves E, hence it
preserves a nonzero denominator. E=0 remains undefined. These arguments establish
the relevant algebraic properties; finite synthetic checks do not establish
scientific effects or a new production validation gate.

On a single reciprocal pair, the null gives the reciprocal graph and the
two-loop graph with equal mass. Support and occurrence fractions are undefined
in the latter case: their undefined mass is 1/2, and their unconditional means
and contrasts remain unavailable. Conditioning those outcomes away would invent
a different null. Defining a new zero-denominator convention would require a new
candidate/version, rather than repairing these consumed definitions after the
results. Budget allocation has a defined exact null mean of 1/2 here, with
contrast +1/2. The separate-loop control has contrast −1/2. These are synthetic
signed witnesses, not discoveries about rewriting rules.

Budget allocation still has 390 degenerate exact-null fixture instances, 420
informative instances and six empty-graph undefined instances among the 816
exact controls. The other candidates' complete status counts remain in the
[receipt](r06a.json). A defined denominator does not guarantee an informative
null. No constant null was promoted, and no random null sampling was run.

The draft intervention outcome compares subsequent score changes over 50
successful events: `(target_50-target_0)-(sham_50-sham_0)`, with initial values
measured immediately after the respective interventions. It intentionally
excludes instantaneous score change and is not the treatment effect on the raw
endpoint level. It includes scheduler/competition effects and establishes no
valid sham or local causal identification. Terminal-before-horizon, censored,
unsupported or undefined endpoints keep a null pair; any missing pair prevents
an aggregate mean and all registered parents remain counted. No interventions
were simulated to check this arithmetic.
